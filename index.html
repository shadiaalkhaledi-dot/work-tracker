1 · The journey of an item
Something happens
meeting · Teams · email · site "+ item"→ Check-in with Claude
folded into the vault, source named→ Lands as a card
one home: Program → Package · one question: its thread→ A step gets ticked
agency recomputes · the thread's stage follows→ Done
✅ Settled → History

One item = one workstream. Nothing about whose turn it is ever gets typed — tick a step and the item moves itself. That is why it can't go stale.

2 · One home, many lenses

Every item lives in exactly one place (Program → Package) and belongs to exactly one question (its thread). Everything else is a lens — a dial that cuts across all programs at once:

agencyderived, never written. Whose turn it is — mine (do it) · shared (check in) · theirs (chase). Read from the next unchecked step: WAIT … means theirs, any other verb means yours
courtwho the item is with — the counterparty. Stable and relational. It does not claim whose turn it is
ask →an unanswered question out to someone. Additive — it never moves the item
then →who it goes to once your part is finished — the handoff, not the current holder
withthey execute, accountability stays yours. Sets agency to shared — the ball bounces, so the move is check in, not chase
horizontoday · week · sprint (rides the package's next submission) · watch (deliberately unclocked — this is what exempts an item from the date and staleness checks)
impactwhy it matters, not how much: issued-wrong · code · cost · decision · unrecorded · owner-facing (the first four are read from the item’s own words; decision and owner-facing are declared-only; a bare high renders as UNSIGNALLED). Absent means normal
actionthe kind of work: model · review · coordinate · admin
disciplinewhat trade the item is about — the subject, nothing else
categorywhat kind of thing it is: decisions · meetings · coordination · documentation · drawings · rfi
3 · How a card earns its look

Priority is computed, never hand-set — urgency crossed with importance, worked out when the data is generated:

normalcarries an impact signal
due ≤ 3 days, or picked for todayhighhigh
due ≤ 10 days, or rides a submission ≤ 10 daysmedhigh
no clocklowmed

An impact signal lifts one tier rather than overriding — so important-and-not-urgent stops being invisible without shouting as loudly as a deadline that has already arrived.

When something sits in someone else's court, the card says what you are waiting for and how long. That is derived too, read from the open WAIT … step, the Next line, or an outstanding ask — you don't fill in a field.

5 · The four fields that say whose move it is

They look redundant and are not. Collapsing any two of them loses something the board then cannot tell you. Only these are typed — agency is worked out from them.

court:: XWho the item is with. Relational and stable — it is what you prep a meeting against. It does not claim whose turn it is.
ask:: XAn unanswered question out to X. Additive: it never moves the item, it only records that something is outstanding.
then:: XWho picks it up after your part. The handoff, not the current holder.
with:: XExecution sits with X; accountability stays yours. This is what makes an item shared — and why shared work is a check-in after 3 days, not a chase after 5. (delegate:: still parses, but write with::.)

Whose turn it is is never typed. It is read from the next unticked step: a step beginning WAIT … means theirs, any other verb means yours, and with:: overrides both. Tick a step and it moves — which is why it cannot go stale, and why there is no field to forget to update.

One more clock, and it is not a date: horizon:: says what kind of clock an item is on — today, week, sprint (it rides its package's next submission), or watch. Watch is deliberately unclocked, and it is what exempts an item from due-date and staleness nagging. The 📅 date is the only actual deadline.

6 · Matters — why something jumps the queue

One job: a signal lifts an item one priority tier. It is not a topic — cat:: and disc:: already say what a thing is. It is the reason this should come first when its date does not say so. Absent is normal, and the common case.

issued-wrong
The drawings are already out and they are wrong. Someone is pricing or building from them — rework or claim exposure.
code
Code, AHJ or life-safety. Externally adjudicated, and not tradeable against schedule.
cost
Money moves if this changes or slips — a PCN, an SI, or a claim.
decision
A fork other work is waiting behind. Until it is made, the work cannot start.
unrecorded
Agreed, but it exists nowhere on paper. Decays the longer it goes unwritten.
owner-facing
YYC or SteerCo has seen this, or will. Their expectation is set by it.

The first four are read automatically from the item’s own words, like agency and waitingFor — you never type them, and a derived badge renders outlined so you can tell it from one you declared. Writing an explicit impact:: in the vault always wins. decision and owner-facing are declared only: the text does not settle them, and a guessed reason reads as a fact.

Retired: critical-path (PCL owns the schedule — you cannot verify it from here, and what it really meant now lives in waiting for and the thread’s blocks), ripple (true of 30 of 58 open items, so it ranked nothing) and safety (it read as site safety; the real category is code).

7 · "Coordinating with" — the derived lens

The Discipline filter shows what an item is about. "Coordinating with" answers a different question: which discipline is this item with? It's never tagged — the item's court names a person or a firm → Stakeholders says what hat they wear → the item lands in that group. This is about the counterparty, not whose turn it is — that is agency, and an item can be yours to move while still sitting with Mechanical. Use it to batch conversations: before the mechanical call, everything with a mechanical counterparty is one list.

item: court = Greg Pink→ Stakeholders: Greg = EL · SMP→ shows under "Coordinating with → Electrical"
8 · RFIs — their own little machine
open
contractor raises→ answered
design responds→ reopened
if unsettled — rev .0 → .1→ closed
scope/cost change → PCN or SI

This is the RFI's own lifecycle and it runs alongside whether the item is still live, not instead of it — an RFI can be answered and still leave you work to do.

Addenda get the same treatment: the date lives in Milestones, the contents in the addendum register on the Progress tab — one card per upcoming addendum, every item tagged addendum:: gathered under it.

RFIs live inside their package and in the RFI Log register. Concourse B's run in Onware, Centralized's on the Planner board. The package's phase decides the closing instrument: in bid the answer rides an Addendum; after IFC (construction) a scope/cost answer closes via PCN or SI — the chip on each package header says which world you're in.

9 · Two people, one board

Everything you do here is an op in a shared queue — a letter, a ticked step, a state, a completion, a new sheet. The other person sees it within about eight seconds and it arrives with your name on it. Set your name once: the chip in the check-in queue header. Without it your captures land on their screen anonymous.

Shared is not saved. A capture is visible to everyone the moment you type it, and it is still only a capture — it carries a P until a check-in folds it into the vault. The vault is the record; this page is a view of it plus whatever has not been folded yet.

Two consequences worth knowing before they surprise you. Clearing the queue clears it for everyone — only do it after a check-in has taken the work. And two people editing the same field within a few seconds is last-writer-wins; there is no lock. In practice you divide the sheet list rather than negotiate it.

If the header chip reads Sync error, nothing is lost — the site falls back to this browser alone and keeps working. If it reads Local only, no shared store is configured and your captures are going nowhere but here.
